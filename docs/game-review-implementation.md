# Game review: detailed human comparison

Detailed review screens the full main line with Stockfish (eight candidate moves), confirms unstable classifications, and compares all eligible non-obvious decisions with Maia-3. Forced moves, conservative recapture cases, terminal positions, verified tablebase positions and already decided wins do not feed the strength profile. Frequent opening moves receive reduced weight. Quick review retains at most six selected Maia decisions.

Each detailed decision is sampled at the player's level and up to six neighboring levels, in 200-point steps over ±600. Levels are clipped to Maia's supported 1100–3000 range and deduplicated. The opponent's chosen rating remains fixed. The actual sampled level and partial coverage are stored; unavailable inferences never become zero-probability evidence. Separate budgets (1200 neural calls and ten minutes after initialization) guard very long games and slow devices.

Detailed review also checks repertoire moves with Stockfish; membership in a saved opening is not proof of move quality. Accurate theory moves receive less importance, while errors retain their weight. Quick review retains its repertoire shortcut.

Stockfish is released before Maia allocates the model. After all neural comparisons and opponent replies, Maia is released and Stockfish restarts once to verify plausible alternatives that were absent from its initial candidates. Up to eight alternatives per position and 240 additional queries are considered, balancing positions in successive rounds and seeking 90% probability coverage across all sampled levels. Unassessed mass remains in the upper bound on sound choices; it is never assumed bad. A rare move is not a difficult find unless conservative good-move mass, a verified tempting mistake, objective quality and the obvious-move filter all support that conclusion.

The report separates three questions:

- **Decision quality:** an explicitly named Stockfish index, calculated as the importance-weighted mean of `100 × max(0, 1 − impact / 35)` on assessed non-obvious, non-decided moves. This is not Lichess accuracy or Elo.
- **Maia choice profile:** relative weighted likelihood across commonly sampled levels on all usable profiles, including mistakes, invariant choices and style patterns. Selecting only quality-aligned monotone choices would bias the match toward the grid edges. Higher-level likelihood is a strength signal only for accurate moves; a lower-level pattern is a weakness signal only for an objective miss. Non-monotonic patterns remain style evidence. A closest tested level requires eight profiled decisions, at least three quality-supported signals, sufficient total weight and separation between model levels. No hidden rating prior or confidence interval is added. This resemblance is experimental and is not a calibrated estimate of a person's Elo. A best match at the grid edge is explicitly marked.
- **Opponent interaction:** consecutive errors and replies are linked. A reply losing fewer than two impact points preserves an offered chance; returning at least half the benefit and at least five points is a substantially missed chance. These events are not summed again into quality loss. Demanding replies require a conservative upper bound of 30% sound-response mass at the opponent's rating. Counts distinguish the opportunities faced, exploited, offered and escaped.

Per-move details expose Elo samples, played-move likelihood and the checked quality of six leading human alternatives. Review moments retain chronological display and link missed opportunities back to the preceding opponent move, with a verified Stockfish continuation where available.

Validation: `npm run verify:analysis` covers rating boundaries, wide-grid coverage, rare/style signals, objective quality gates, conservative difficulty proof, opportunity links, cancellation, model failures, the quick-mode budget, WDL and safe winning simplifications. Browser verification additionally exercises real workers and the report UI.
